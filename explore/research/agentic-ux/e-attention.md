# Attention is the production limit

[inferred] Orrery should coordinate demands on the engineer, rather than make every working agent more visible.
[sourced] Interruption studies distinguish delayed return, memory reconstruction, stress, and errors, rather than establishing one universal switching penalty [1, 2, 5].
[inferred] The useful unit of supervision is a bounded decision with evidence, an owner, and a deadline, not an agent transcript.
[inferred] Keep routine progress peripheral, deliver decisions at human breakpoints, and reserve interruption for consequential harm that cannot wait.
[inferred] Returning after an hour should open a checked handover of changes and unresolved choices, not sixty minutes of replay.
[sourced] Practitioner reports range from three or four sustainable threads to eight [23, 24]. [inferred] The reviewed evidence does not establish a population limit.
[guess] The strongest concept is a plant that promises when it next needs you and slows new work when your review capacity is exhausted.

## What interruption research actually licenses

[sourced] Mark and colleagues found interrupted participants finished the measured email work faster without a significant quality difference, while reporting more stress, frustration, time pressure, and effort [1].
[inferred] A faster approval queue could therefore conceal a worse workday.
[inferred] Orrery should ask whether users understood and trusted accepted changes, not whether agents spent less time waiting.

[sourced] Iqbal and Horvitz describe chains of diversion after alerts, including visits to other applications before resuming the primary task [2].
[inferred] An alert's cost includes the path it opens, not only the seconds spent answering it.
[inferred] A decision card should resolve in place and return the engineer to the prior territory, rather than opening several unrelated feeds.

[sourced] Parnin and Rugaber's journal abstract reports 10,000 recorded sessions from 86 programmers and a survey of 414 programmers [5].
[sourced] Only 10% of sessions resumed programming within one minute, and only 7% involved no navigation before editing [5].
[inferred] These are observations about resumption behavior, not proof that every notification costs fifteen minutes.
[inferred] The familiar twenty-three-minute claim must not become a universal developer penalty or a marketing calculator.

[sourced] Altmann and Trafton's memory-for-goals model uses activation, interference, and associative priming rather than an infallible mental goal stack [4].
[sourced] The model requires useful cues to be available when a goal is suspended and when it is retrieved [4].
[inferred] Stable authored machine positions are useful departure and return anchors.
[inferred] A saved view alone is insufficient if the engineer cannot recover what they were trying to establish.

[sourced] Leroy and Glomb found that a brief ready-to-resume plan reduced attention residue under anticipated time pressure across four studies [6].
[inferred] Before switching, offer one editable sentence such as "Next, confirm retries preserve the payment idempotency key."
[inferred] The human's intention must remain distinct from an agent-generated guess about that intention.

[sourced] Iqbal and Bailey's notification system reduced frustration and reaction time compared with immediate delivery, with average deferral around 1.5 minutes [3].
[sourced] Their breakpoint models struggled to differentiate breakpoint types [3].
[inferred] Orrery should begin with explicit checkpoints, not claim to infer mental availability from a keyboard pause.
[inferred] An agent finishing a test is an agent breakpoint, not necessarily the engineer's breakpoint.

[sourced] Flow research describes just-manageable challenge, clear proximal goals, and immediate feedback about progress [7].
[sourced] Newport advises that breaks during deep work should avoid unfinished professional obligations that redirect attention [8].
[inferred] Supervising six open-ended threads is a different activity from concentrating on one design problem.
[inferred] Orrery can protect a chosen activity, but should not advertise a scientifically measured "flow mode."

## Calm awareness needs a trustworthy background

[sourced] Weiser and Brown describe technology moving between the periphery and center of attention [9].
[sourced] Natalie Jeremijenko's Dangling String made network activity visible and audible through a motor-driven plastic string [9].
[inferred] Its useful lesson is a learnable normal condition whose change attracts attention, not that software should always move.

[sourced] MIT's ambientROOM communicated background activity with light, shadow, sound, airflow, and water movement, with physical controls for bringing information forward [10].
[sourced] Ambient Devices' Energy Orb maps demand conditions to color and pulses during high demand [11].
[sourced] The Jenkins Hue monitor implements aggregated build states rather than one light event per log line [13].
[inferred] Orrery could summarize demands on the engineer in one stable peripheral instrument while preserving detailed inspection on the plant.

[sourced] Peep maps network events and states into an ecology of natural sounds that administrators compare with a normally functioning network [12].
[inferred] Agent sonification should communicate a small aggregate condition, not give each bot its own chirp.
[guess] A change from quiet mechanical rhythm to a single muted knock could announce a newly urgent decision without requiring a glance.
[inferred] Sound needs an equally effective visual alternative and must default to off in shared rooms.

[sourced] Information radiators put current team information in a visible place for at-a-glance reading [14].
[inferred] Orrery inverts the team wall display by radiating the state of many workers to one decision-maker.
[inferred] The second monitor earns its place when not looking is safe.
[inferred] Q15's stillness rule should remain intact, and render sleep must never imply that telemetry or agent monitoring has stopped.
[guess] A small freshness timestamp and explicit disconnected state would make silence easier to trust than decorative belt motion.

[inferred] The action inbox needs a lifecycle distinct from unread messages.
[guess] Requests move through new, evidence requested, deferred until a stated condition, resolved, and superseded states.
[inferred] A superseded request should retain its replacement and reason, so agents cannot quietly erase unresolved decisions.
[inferred] Completed work belongs in the digest unless acceptance actually requires a human.
[guess] The user should be able to explain why each remaining item requires them without opening a transcript.

## How many agents can a developer supervise?

[sourced] Boris Cherny reported five parallel terminal sessions on January 2, 2026, using numbered tabs and system notifications for required input [22].
[sourced] Schipper reports four to eight agents, constant waiting demands beyond six, and degraded decisions beyond roughly eight [23].
[sourced] His workflow relies on detailed feature designs, checkpoints, and verification rather than eight unstructured chats [23].
[sourced] Osmani reports a typical personal ceiling around three or four threads and argues that scope, session duration, and available energy change that ceiling [24].
[inferred] These reports describe different work arrangements, not conflicting measurements of a universal limit.

[sourced] In the HN discussion of Schipper's setup, a commenter reports finding two or three focused agents more reliable than six to eight because integration overhead consumes gains [25].
[inferred] This is useful testimony about a failure mode, not a representative developer survey.
[sourced] Steinberger describes building speed limited largely by inference and hard thinking, while reading relatively little generated code [26].
[inferred] His report is a counterexample to treating constant line-by-line supervision as everyone's preferred workflow.

[sourced] Anthropic's 2025 internal study surveyed 132 engineers and researchers and conducted 53 interviews [27].
[sourced] It reports increased breadth and productivity alongside concerns about deeper competence and effective supervision [27].
[sourced] Anthropic's October 16, 2026 report describes interactive Claude sessions spending around 80% of wall-clock session time waiting for human input [28].
[sourced] The report derives idle time by subtracting active agent work from session wall time, and includes an engineer comparing equally insistent requests to children needing either a juice box opened or treatment for bleeding [28].
[inferred] That idle fraction is not an inefficiency target, since it does not establish whether waiting was harmful or intentional.
[inferred] These internal observations from one AI-intensive company are not a controlled estimate of general supervision capacity.

[sourced] METR's early-2025 randomized study found experienced maintainers took 19% longer with the tested AI tools, despite believing they were faster [29].
[sourced] Its February 2026 update says later estimates are unreliable because of selection effects and difficulties measuring time when developers use concurrent agents [30].
[inferred] Neither result establishes today's optimum agent count.
[inferred] Orrery should measure review demand, accepted outcomes, defects, return time, and fatigue together.
[guess] A personal concurrency setting learned from explicit review budgets is more defensible than a product-wide "eight agents" default.

## Ideas Orrery could steal or invert

### 1. Safe to leave

[guess] The user sees "Safe to leave until 14:30" beside the plant, with the next expected decision and its assumptions.
[guess] They choose a return time, approve permitted work, and let bots continue within existing checks and budgets.
[inferred] This addresses vigilance caused by uncertainty about what might happen while the engineer looks away.
[sourced] Osmani's bounded sessions and HSE's handover contingencies provide precedents [19, 24].
[inferred] The agentic inversion is a negotiated period of human absence rather than another availability indicator.
[guess] The risk is false reassurance when agents discover unexpected hazards.
[inferred] The promise must mean "no unapproved consequential action," not "nothing will go wrong."
[guess] A newly discovered boundary violation ends the promise with an explicit reason.

### 2. Deliver at my checkpoint

[guess] A pending marker says "Two decisions held until you finish this review."
[guess] The engineer can choose "Show now," set a latest delivery time, or finish the review to release the batch.
[inferred] This prevents one agent's convenient completion time from determining the human's work rhythm.
[sourced] Defer-to-breakpoint systems reduced interruption burden in Iqbal and Bailey's studies [3].
[inferred] The new application coordinates requests across independent agents around the human's current unit of work.
[guess] The risk is starving blocked work or mistaking inactivity for permission to interrupt.
[inferred] Use explicit checkpoints first, retain a maximum delay, and disclose what may become late.
[inferred] Irreversible harm remains a separately explained exception.

### 3. The return brief

[guess] Returning after an hour opens "Since you left" with changed outcomes, current risk, requested actions, and contingencies.
[guess] Selecting a change overlays the exact machine and source evidence, with an option to recover the departure note.
[inferred] This replaces transcript reconstruction with a checked transfer of responsibility.
[sourced] HSE requires preparation, exchange, and cross-checking, while I-PASS includes severity, summary, actions, contingency planning, and receiver synthesis [19, 21].
[sourced] The nine-hospital I-PASS intervention was associated with 23% fewer medical errors and 30% fewer preventable adverse events [20].
[inferred] Those gains belong to the full clinical intervention, not a summary card.
[inferred] Agentic novelty comes from combining designed, declared, and observed changes into one handover.
[guess] The risk is a fluent summary concealing missing evidence.
[inferred] Preserve disagreement, freshness, and direct drill-down instead of generating a reassuring narrative.

### 4. One symptom, one decision

[guess] Five bots report one card saying "Payment verification is blocked by an expired sandbox credential."
[guess] The engineer sees affected work, evidence, one permitted response, and a separate list of contributing reports.
[inferred] This solves an alarm flood where independent agents demand the same intervention.
[sourced] Google SRE favors urgent, actionable, user-visible symptoms, and rejects noisy individual-machine paging [15, 16].
[inferred] The new unit is the shared human decision, even when multiple agents and tools encounter it.
[guess] The risk is grouping unrelated failures and hiding a second cause.
[inferred] Label uncertain correlations, permit separation, and keep raw reports recoverable.
[inferred] Do not suppress distinct security or data-loss concerns merely because they occur together.

### 5. Review capacity limits production

[guess] A gauge shows "Three decisions ready, about forty minutes of review" instead of celebrating twelve active bots.
[guess] The engineer gives the session a review budget, and further tasks wait when predicted review demand exceeds it.
[inferred] This addresses excess production that arrives faster than the human can understand it.
[sourced] Schipper describes constant pending work at higher agent counts, and Osmani reports better usable output with fewer well-reviewed threads [23, 24].
[inferred] The agentic change is admission control based on human review rather than compute capacity.
[guess] Time estimates may be wrong and create another misleading productivity score.
[inferred] Show a range, expose its basis, allow override, and never reduce evidence requirements to fit the budget.
[inferred] Holding a task is preferable to silently accepting its output.

### 6. Decisions with an interruption deadline

[guess] Every queue card shows "Why you," "What happens if this waits," and "Latest safe response."
[guess] The user can defer, request evidence, authorize an option, or return the task to the agent.
[inferred] This distinguishes actual blocked work from an agent asking for reassurance.
[sourced] HSE requires every alarm to have a defined response, while Google distinguishes pages, tickets, and email [15, 18].
[inferred] K17's choice-risk ordering remains unchanged.
[inferred] Delivery timing is a separate policy, because reversal cost and harm from delay answer different questions.
[inferred] The novelty is that agents must explain their claim on human attention before receiving it.
[guess] Agents may exaggerate urgency or invent confidence.
[inferred] Derive deadlines from explicit policy and observed conditions, and show unknown confidence rather than a fabricated percentage.

### 7. A quiet instrument for human demand

[guess] A brass edge instrument remains static during ordinary progress and changes shape when a response becomes due.
[guess] Clicking it opens the highest-risk waiting choice, while optional sound announces only a meaningful escalation.
[inferred] This serves the second-monitor case without animating every bot.
[sourced] The Dangling String, ambientROOM, Energy Orb, Peep, and CI lights establish peripheral state communication [9, 10, 11, 12, 13].
[inferred] Orrery's inversion is to encode aggregate demand on the human rather than aggregate machine activity.
[guess] The risk is an ambiguous signal that encourages compulsive checking.
[inferred] Each state needs a stable meaning, redundant text and shape, and a visible freshness indicator.
[inferred] Normal background work should produce no celebratory sounds.

### 8. Leave a thought, not a transcript

[guess] Before entering another territory, the engineer can save one sentence about the unresolved question and next test.
[guess] On return, Orrery restores that sentence beside the relevant machine and shows changes since it was written.
[inferred] This preserves the human's plan independently of agents' summaries.
[sourced] Memory-for-goals theory and ready-to-resume studies support preserving retrieval cues and a plan for return [4, 6].
[inferred] The agentic distinction is an explicit boundary between what the human intended and what workers subsequently did.
[guess] The risk is turning every switch into clerical work.
[inferred] Make the note optional, preserve existing context automatically, and ask only when a meaningful unfinished decision would otherwise be lost.

## What to avoid

[inferred] Avoid a global unread count that mixes milestones, routine tool output, questions, and urgent harm.
[sourced] Anthropic's equal-urgency babysitting account illustrates how unlike requests compete for identical attention [28].
[inferred] A queue entry needs a decision, an owner, and consequences, not merely a new message.

[inferred] Avoid wall-to-wall motion and constant completion chimes.
[sourced] Calm technology is about controllable movement between peripheral and central attention, not perpetual sensory stimulation [9].
[inferred] Animation density is not a reliable statement of system health or user value.

[inferred] Avoid blanket silence without a predictable next contact.
[sourced] Fitz and colleagues found three daily notification batches improved several self-reported outcomes, while eliminating notifications increased anxiety and fear of missing out [17].
[inferred] This smartphone result supports testing predictable contact, not copying a three-times-daily schedule into development.

[inferred] Avoid pages for every failed test, retry, or disconnected bot.
[sourced] Google warns that noisy pages teach people to skim or ignore alerts and can mask real incidents [15].
[sourced] HSE identifies a five-hour barrage of alarms before the Milford Haven refinery incident as evidence of poor alarm-system design [18].
[inferred] Correlate and explain failures before demanding intervention, without hiding uncertainty.

[inferred] Avoid treating "acknowledged" as "approved" or "verified."
[sourced] HSE handover guidance requires the incoming person to cross-check information, and I-PASS explicitly includes receiver synthesis [19, 21].
[inferred] Dismissing a brief must not approve a budget change, erase a failed check, or transfer responsibility silently.

[inferred] Avoid maximizing agent occupancy, treating faster approval as success, or setting concurrency from a famous practitioner's terminal count.
[sourced] Mark's participants worked faster with greater strain, and METR found perceived speed differed from measured task completion in its tested setting [1, 29].
[guess] The bakeoff should compare these concepts using missed critical decisions, review defects, return-to-understanding time, and end-of-session fatigue.
[inferred] A concept that lets an engineer safely ignore the plant for forty minutes is more promising than one that makes forty minutes of activity compelling to watch.
